Ch. 01

The Shelf

Point Meringo Listen at a folder of M4Bs and MP3s, sign into your Audiobookshelf server, or do both. Local files and server books land on the same shelf, sorted the way you actually listen: in-progress at the top, recently added next, finished in the long tail. Browse by author, series or what you played last.

A small badge marks which copy is local and which is on the server, for the listener who cares about the difference. Everyone else just sees their library — calm, current, and exactly where they left it.

Ch. 02

Audiobookshelf

A premium, paid native Android client for Audiobookshelf, built in Compose with no web-shell seams. Real two-way position sync. Real offline downloads. Multi-library. And real conflict resolution: listening done offline is checked against your server before it is sent, and if another device has since got a minute or more further — “your Audiobookshelf server is ahead of this device by 35 min” — you choose which place to keep.

Your bearer token is stored encrypted in the Android Keystore, and your device talks straight to your server — nothing proxies through us. Downloads land in the same on-disk layout the official client uses, so your files migrate cleanly.

Ch. 03

The voice EQ

Seven presets built for the situations spoken word actually lives in — Neutral, Deep Voice, Tame Bright, Phone Speaker, Clarity, Late Night, and Subway, which pairs an EQ shift with a real downward compressor so narration sits steady when the train doesn’t.

Add a one-tap Noisy-room boost (Subway, Stroller, Kitchen) for the moments the world gets loud. It’s the same 64-bit double-precision engine that ships in Meringo, the sister music player, re-tuned for narration.

The Voice EQ Workbench

Want the controls, not just the presets? The Workbench gives you all ten bands by hand, with a live curve. Paste an AutoEQ profile and Meringo Listen maps it onto the cascade. A hand-tuned curve isn’t saved: it lasts until you pick a preset or close the app.

Ch. 04

Per-narrator memory

Find the preset that makes a narrator sound right, and Meringo Listen remembers it. Start another book by the same reader and it asks: “Last time you listened to Eleanor Voss. You used the Late Night preset. Restore it for this book?”

The memory lives entirely on your device — no preset cloud, no account, no telemetry. Tap Restore and stop thinking about EQ for the rest of the book.

Ch. 05

Speed

Meringo Listen changes speed with Media3’s Sonic time-stretch — the Sonic algorithm (originally Bill Cox’s), which shifts tempo while holding pitch. A narrator keeps their natural voice, and their consonants stay crisp, all the way up to 3×.

Pitch correction is on at every speed, so voices never turn into chipmunks. And at 1.0× the time-stretch is a clean passthrough — nothing touches the audio you didn’t ask it to, and the route badge says so.

Ch. 06

Listen anywhere

Pause for a meeting and Meringo Listen smart-rewinds to the last natural pause when you come back — far enough to find the sentence, not so far you re-hear a paragraph. Force-stop the app or reboot the phone: the queue, the position, the speed are all exactly where you left them, and it waits for a press of play before any sound: yours, or the one some cars send as they connect.

Drift off with the Sleep Mode envelope — a gentle fade that can wait for the end of the chapter, and a screen that dims as a timed countdown runs out — that never nags. Drop a bookmark on a passage worth keeping; it stays on your device. Android Auto and chapter-aware Bluetooth controls come along for the drive.

Ch. 07

Honest audio

Tap the route badge and Meringo Listen tells you the truth about your audio path. When nothing is touching the bytes, it reads BIT-EXACT. Turn on speed, a compressor, or volume scaling and the badge decorates rather than lies — “Speed 2.5×”, “LRC Subway”, “Scaled −2 dB”.

For most audiobooks this is a quiet detail. For the listener who imported a 24-bit / 96 kHz audio-drama production, it’s the whole point — the same honest engine, and the same route badge, that ships in Meringo, the sister music player.

Is Meringo Listen for you?

We’d rather you read this and bounce than download and refund.

Built for you if…

  • You own your audiobooks — locally, on a server, or both.
  • You self-host Audiobookshelf and have squinted at the official Android client.
  • You keep an M4B / MP3 archive and you’re tired of an interface from 2014.
  • You’ve ever rewound because a notification or a noisy train swallowed a sentence.
  • You’ve ever wished a player would remember the EQ preset you chose for a narrator.
  • You’re on Android 13 or newer, on a phone or a DAP.

Not for you if…

  • You primarily listen to Audible — AAX is DRM and we don’t decode it. Ever.
  • You want a catalog or a subscription to discover books in. There isn’t one.
  • You need Dropbox, Google Drive, or OneDrive sources. Server or local files only.
  • You’re an F-Droid purist who won’t pay for closed-source. (Try Voice — it’s great.)
  • You’re on iOS. Prologue, SoundLeaf, ShelfPlayer and AudioBooth already serve you.
  • You want streaks, leaderboards, or gamification. One quiet screen of counts (books finished, hours into your library) is the whole stats surface.

Built by one person, on the same engine as Meringo Music. The rule is the same: claim less than the code does, lead with what it won’t do, and never make the listener pay rent for software they already bought.

Questions

Can Meringo Listen play my Audible books?

No. Audible’s AAX files are DRM-protected and Meringo Listen does not decode them — that’s a deliberate, permanent choice, not a roadmap item. It’s built for books you own DRM-free: downloaded from a retailer like Libro.fm, ripped yourself, or served from your own Audiobookshelf library. If your library lives in Audible, stay with Audible.

Do I need an Audiobookshelf server?

No. Point Meringo Listen at a folder of audiobooks and everything works — playback, chapters, voice EQ, speed, bookmarks, sleep timer. Audiobookshelf is a first-class option for people who self-host, not a requirement. Local files and server books sit on the same shelf.

Which formats does it play?

M4B, MP3 (including ID3v2 CHAP chapter frames), M4A, AAC, OGG/Opus, FLAC, and WAV. Chapter navigation works from container metadata and MP3 CHAP frames.

Is there a subscription?

No. $14.99 once, then it’s yours — after a 14-day free trial of the full app. If you reinstall or switch phones, your purchase restores automatically against your Google account. No ads, no premium tier behind a second purchase.

Does it track me?

No analytics SDK, no ad SDK, no engagement metrics, no listening data sent anywhere. Crash reports through Sentry are opt-in and off by default, and your server URLs are redacted before any crash payload leaves the device. Apart from Google Play’s own billing library, which handles the purchase, your Audiobookshelf server and optional Sentry are the only outbound connections.

How does it compare to Smart AudioBook Player or Voice?

Smart AudioBook Player is a long-running local-files player. Voice is excellent, free and FOSS, deliberately minimal — with no EQ. Meringo Listen is the paid, premium-aesthetic option with a 64-bit voice EQ, per-narrator memory, and first-class Audiobookshelf. Different trade-offs; all three can exist.

Is this the official Audiobookshelf app?

No. Meringo Listen is an independent third-party client and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Audiobookshelf project. The official client is free and will keep existing; this is a paid, native-Compose alternative for listeners who want a premium client. Your device talks directly to your server — nothing proxies through us.

Can I stream from Dropbox, Google Drive, or OneDrive?

No. The source list is Audiobookshelf and local folders (via Android’s Storage Access Framework).

Will there be an iOS version?

Not in the next twelve months. iOS Audiobookshelf listeners are already well served by Prologue, SoundLeaf, ShelfPlayer and AudioBooth. Meringo Listen is Android-first by deliberate choice.

Does it work with Android Auto?

Yes — bookshelf browse and playback controls are wired through Android Auto, with chapter-aware transport. A double-press on a Bluetooth headset skips to the next chapter, not the next file.

Privacy Policy

Last updated 2026-10-08 · Meringo Labs LLC

The short version

Meringo Listen is built local-first. It has no analytics, no ads, and no engagement tracking. The only data that ever leaves your device is (1) traffic to the Audiobookshelf server you choose to connect, (2) optional crash reports, which are off by default, and (3) what Google Play’s billing library exchanges with Google when you buy the app or restore your purchase. We never see your books, your listening, or your identity.

Your audiobooks & local files

When you add a folder, Android’s Storage Access Framework grants Meringo Listen read access to only that folder. Your audio never leaves the device — there is no upload, no scan sent anywhere, no cover-art lookup that transmits your files.

Audiobookshelf

If you connect an Audiobookshelf server, your device talks directly to that server to stream, download, and sync your position. The connection is encrypted when your server’s address starts with https://. A server on plain http://, common on a home network, works too, but that connection is not encrypted. Your access token is stored encrypted in the Android Keystore. Nothing is proxied through Meringo Labs, and your server’s address is never shared with us.

Crash reports (opt-in)

Crash reporting via Sentry is opt-in and off by default. If you turn it on and the app crashes, the report contains the stack trace and the app’s version, plus the state of the device at that moment: its model, Android version and build, language and time zone, battery level and whether it was charging, free memory and storage, connection type (Wi-Fi or mobile data), screen size, the permissions the app holds, the screen that was open, and whether the app came from Google Play.

Sentry gives each installation a random ID so that repeat crashes from one device are counted together. It is not your advertising ID, and it is not tied to your name or any account. Sentry is set not to store IP addresses, so reports carry no location. Your server URLs are redacted before any payload leaves the device, and reports never include book titles, listening positions, or account tokens.

You can turn crash reports off at any time. To request deletion of any crash data, write to support@meringo.app.

Purchases through Google Play

Buying Meringo Listen and restoring your purchase go through Google Play. Google’s billing library is part of the app and communicates with Google to do this, under Google’s Privacy Policy. For our accounts, Google gives us a record of each sale, including the order number, the price, and the buyer’s country, region and postal code.

What we never collect

No advertising ID, no device fingerprint, no account, no contacts, no location, no microphone, no listening history sent off-device. There is no recommendation engine and no social feed by design.

Your rights

Uninstalling Meringo Listen removes all local data (folder permissions are released; Keystore entries are dropped). EU/EEA users may lodge a complaint with their national data-protection authority; California residents may contact the California Attorney General. For any privacy question, write to support@meringo.app.

Open-source notice

Meringo Listen bundles a Meringo-built FFmpeg derivative (libavcodec, libavutil, libswresample) under the GNU LGPL v2.1 or later. Build configuration, version, license texts, and a written offer for the source are available in the app under Settings → Open-Source Licenses, or by emailing support@meringo.app.
